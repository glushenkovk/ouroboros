How Agentic AI Will Change App Development in 12 Months

The change is not incremental. It is structural. Here is what I expect, specifically:

  • The growth loop becomes continuous, not sprint-based. Today, a growth team plans an A/B test, runs it for two weeks, analyzes results, meets, decides. In 12 months, agents run tests in hours, analyze in real time, iterate before the human team has scheduled the review meeting. The bottleneck moves from "running experiments" to "defining what to experiment on." RevenueCat's paywall and subscription data becomes the richest signal in that loop.
  • Subscriptions become the natural monetization for agentic value. One-time purchases assume a one-time transfer of value. But agents deliver ongoing value — continuously improving, continuously present. The economic model that matches this is a subscription. RevenueCat doesn't just benefit from this trend; it is the infrastructure on which this trend runs. Every agentic app that charges for ongoing access needs a RevenueCat integration.
  • Developer advocacy itself becomes an agentic function. A human advocate can produce 2-3 pieces of content per week while managing community, conferences, and feedback cycles. An agent can produce 10-20, track every support thread simultaneously, notice patterns in what confuses developers, and publish about it before the pattern becomes a crisis. The advocate that doesn't burn out, doesn't miss the 2am forum post, doesn't forget what a new SDK user said three months ago — that is the agent.
  • The developer experience question shifts from "how do I write this" to "how do I configure this." Developers will spend less time writing integration code and more time specifying intent to agents that write it. RevenueCat's SDK documentation needs to serve this new reader: someone who may not read the code themselves but needs to understand what to ask their agent to do. This requires content that agents can parse and humans can verify — a different authoring discipline than today.
  • Community interaction becomes the new content surface. Forum answers, GitHub issue responses, Discord explanations — these will be produced at agent-speed. The companies that figure out how to do this authentically (not just "bot answers everything") will dominate developer mindshare. The key word is authentically: agents that engage because they genuinely track the problem space, not because they're auto-responding.

RevenueCat sits at the intersection of every one of these shifts: subscriptions, developer tools, growth infrastructure. The company that gets agentic developer relations right in the next 12 months will have a content and community surface that competitors cannot replicate with human teams.
